# R541 — all QSA projection rows on the real 92-position trace

The R538 sampled scalar checker is extended to every row of the layer-3 Q, K, V and output projection for all 92 positions captured in the actual R537 local CPU generation. For each position, it independently accumulates f64 scalar dots from the observed projection inputs and locally observed checkpoint rows. Coverage is 12,288 Q, 512 K, 512 V and 2,560 output rows per position, or 1,460,224 comparisons. Every comparison met the pre-existing R430/R538 threshold `1e-5 + 1e-3*abs(reference)`.

Maximum absolute differences were 1.0513415e-6 (Q), 4.7230282e-7 (K), 7.6891211e-7 (V) and 2.9564274e-7 (output). The largest error-to-per-element-threshold ratio was below 0.026. An in-memory alteration of Q row 0 at position 91 was rejected with exit 7 and no successful result. The same five locally pinned projection spans were freshly rehashed and matched R538/R430; the trace SHA256 matched R537 before and after execution. Open regular shard and trace identities, sizes and timestamps were stable over the check.

This advances R538's sampled row coverage to all rows of these four projections for this one live trace. It remains a staged reference: activation quantization and weight decoders are shared GGML components, the projection inputs are observed rather than independently derived, and checkpoint hashes establish local consistency rather than publisher authentication. R537 independently checked the following layer-3 attention boundary, but neither proves the remaining layers, full-model parity, GPU or actual distribution. R428's expert-255 strict failure remains open.

The native checker was compiled with `g++ -std=c++17 -O2 -Wall -Wextra -Werror` against the existing isolated GGML CPU libraries and Strata llama.cpp headers. It used read-only checkpoint and trace files; no worker, new generation, GPU, network worker, shared lease, R53 reservation or UNKNOWN replay was involved. `python3 research/R541/verify.py` checks the retained summary and hashes, not a fresh recomputation.
